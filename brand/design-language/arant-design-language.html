01

Brand

The name and the line

Established

Objects for Considered Spaces.

Always in title case with the full stop. Where a design sets it in capitals (an uppercase label, the seal's ring text), the capitals replace the case.

  • ARANT DESIGN: primary display name ARANT, descriptor DESIGN. Written in capitals in running text.
  • Web arantdesign.com · Instagram @arantdesign.
  • The mark is Balance: two equal stone slabs lean together and hold a pebble. Architectural, tactile and quiet.
  • Positioning, for “about” copy and briefings, not a tagline: A contemporary Indian objects and home-design brand creating refined objects for considered spaces.

Character

Recommended
  • Material-neutral. Current products may use CALSO ONE and other mineral casting materials, but the brand is not a casting brand. Everything must work as well for ceramics, wood, metal, glass, lighting, textiles and furniture.
  • Indian in sensibility, contemporary in expression. Indianness comes from architecture, materials, light and making, never from applied motifs.
  • Considered, warm, tactile, architectural, calm, understated, human. Never flashy, ornate, rustic, cute, sterile, corporate or “craft market”.
02

Principles

Six rules that come from the mark itself: few parts, one idea, even openings, weight and support.

Recommended wording of Established qualities
  1. 1

    One idea, held in balance

    One dominant element per composition; the rest supports it. Asymmetry only when the weights still balance, like the two slabs and the pebble.

  2. 2

    Space is part of the material

    Whitespace is active, like the split in the mark. Never fill space because it is there.

  3. 3

    The object leads

    Products and materials carry the expression; the identity stays small and quiet.

  4. 4

    Structure before ornament

    Alignment, planes, proportion and hairlines instead of decoration.

  5. 5

    Warm through material, not effects

    Warm grounds, real textures and daylight. No gradients, glows or shadows.

  6. 6

    Made to last

    A small, stable vocabulary. Nothing trend-led; nothing tied to one material or category.

When a request is ambiguous, choose the option that is more consistent, clearer, more timeless, more tactile, less decorated and more scalable to future categories, in that order.

03

Visual language

The signature

Established

Warm grounds, Earth Brown type and logo, one Terracotta accent, a geometric sans with an editorial serif, and objects shown on their own surfaces: stone, terrazzo, kraft, paper.

The balances it holds

Recommended
Geometric and organic

Straight grids and square frames around irregular materials and soft light.

Architectural and human

Planes and thresholds, with hands, use and small variations.

Minimal and tactile

Few elements, each with texture: paper, stone, a debossed mark.

Refined and imperfect

Precise type and layout; the objects carry the handmade variation.

Contemporary and timeless

Current in clarity and restraint; no trend effects.

Warm and restrained

Warm neutrals everywhere; accent colour used once.

A quick test: if a composition still looks right with the photograph removed and only the type left, the structure is sound. If it only works because of an effect, it isn't ARANT.

Indian in sensibility

Recommended

Draw on architecture as structure (plinths, thresholds, wall niches, courtyards, stepwell geometry, arches read as openings); the materials of Indian homes (stone floors, lime plaster, terrazzo, brass, clay, cane, handloom); warm daylight with long, soft-edged shadows; making, shown honestly; rupee prices written ₹1,800.

Never use, merely to signal “Indian”: mandalas, lotus motifs, paisley, temple silhouettes, elephants, rangoli or henna patterns, block-print borders, “exotic” colour clashes, or scripts that imitate Devanagari.

Rules

  • Horizontal by default; the others when the space asks for it, never for variety.
  • Works in one colour. Earth Brown or Deep Charcoal on light grounds; the -ivory or -white files on dark. There is no separate small-size or thinned reversed drawing.
  • Clear space: half the ARANT cap height on every side; a quarter of its height for the symbol alone.
  • Minimum size: every opening stays at 0.8 mm or more. In every lockup the narrowest opening is the split in the As of ARANT. Widths are measured on the artwork, not the file's margin (source: brand/identity/README.md):

08

Shape language

Shape needs a reason. Round a corner because the thing is touched or physical; otherwise keep it square.

From the mark

Established

Slabs: two equal, leaning planes with weight. One pebble, held rather than placed. One opening, repeated: the split and the pebble gap share one width. Softened, not rounded, caps and feet. Fixed angles: the N's diagonal is exactly 60°. In the guide, panels, swatches and tables are square with 1 px hairlines; roundness appears only in objects: a round coaster, a circular seal, an arched doorway.

In the interface · radius.*

Established

radius.none · 0

Images, sections, panels, cards, modals, menus. The default.

radius.soft · 2px

Buttons, inputs, selects, small tags. Echoes the logo's caps.

radius.round · 50 %

Only truly circular things: avatar, seal, swatch dot, radio.

  • Cards have no container: an image, then text on the page ground. A needed panel is a flat plate or a hairline box with square corners.
  • Never pill buttons, rounded cards, radii of 8 px and up on large surfaces, blobs or wavy dividers.
  • Packaging: plain rectangular boxes and bands, circular seals, a hang tag with clipped top corners and a round hole. Styling: objects set on planes (a plinth, a stone slab, a shelf, a niche).

Under review

Experimental

Arch-topped image crops for a single editorial moment, such as a journal opener. Not for product grids or UI containers, and not in production until reviewed.

10

Materiality

Refined craftsmanship, never rustic craft.

The mark on material

Established

The guide shows the mark on terrazzo, stone, kraft and paper, as a blind deboss, an engraving and a moulded maker's stamp. Nothing in it is narrower than the split, so it survives a rubber stamp, a deboss and a silicone mould.

Refined, not rustic

Recommended

Yes

  • Precise forms with a soft, finished edge
  • Variation that comes from the process
  • Clean type and generous space around texture
  • Honed stone, sealed plaster, uncoated paper
  • Process shown clearly and calmly

No

  • Rough, lumpy or “distressed” forms
  • Variation added as decoration
  • Hand-lettering, twine and burlap
  • Weathered boards, jute, chalkboard, mason jars
  • “Artisan” theatre, sepia, heavy grain

Rules

  • Brand-level lines, packaging and the website frame never name a material or process. “Cast”, “poured”, “turned” belong in a product's own description.
  • On objects, prefer the symbol or ARANT only; lockups with DESIGN are for print and packaging. Marks go on the underside or back. Wood, metal and glass are engraved from the same files at the same minimum sizes.
  • Texture on screen comes from photographs of real material: at most one textured area per viewport, never behind reading text. No grain overlays, noise, faux stone or torn-paper edges.

Print and placement

Recommended
  • Kraft, uncoated ivory stock and corrugated board. One-colour (Earth, or dark brown on kraft), two-colour (Earth plus Terracotta for one element), rubber stamp or blind deboss. No foil, lamination, spot UV or metallics.
  • The logo small, once per face, with full clear space: a quarter to a third of the face width at most on a box; one small stamp on a shipping carton.
  • Information in Jost; only the product name in Newsreader. Short uppercase headers such as CARE, MATERIAL, SIZE. Proposed minimums: 6 pt legal, 7–8 pt information.
  • Operationally realistic: stock box sizes, short runs, labels and seals applied by hand. No gift range: the standard box with tissue, band and seal, plus a plain card.
  • Retail packs sold in India carry statutory declarations (Legal Metrology rules: packer details, net quantity, MRP, month and year of manufacture, consumer care). Confirm current wording with an advisor; set them legibly in Jost.

Voice and copy

Thoughtful, clear, concise, confident, warm, contemporary, understated.

Established lines

Established

Thank you for giving this object a place in your home.

Also: “Objects for Considered Spaces.” · “Small-batch, hand-finished in India.” · prices written ₹1,800 · British English.

How we write

Recommended
  • Write about form, material, object, space, use, process, detail and intention. One precise adjective rather than three.
  • Materials and processes at product level only, never in brand-level lines, packaging frames or navigation.
  • Only say “handmade” or “small-batch” where it is true. State that hand-finished pieces vary, as a fact.
  • Specific over general: 32 × 18 cm, 1.2 kg. Sentence case. British spelling: colour, centre, grey.
  • Naming: collections take one plain noun from architecture, landscape or form (the Plinth collection); products are collection plus object (Plinth Tray); colour follows as an option. No materials in collection names.
  • No hype, “luxury”, “stunning”, “must-have”, “elevate”, fake scarcity, exclamation marks, emoji or jargon.
