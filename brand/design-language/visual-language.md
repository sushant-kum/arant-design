# Visual language

What an ARANT composition looks like as a whole, and the few graphic elements the system allows. Back to the
[design-language index](README.md).

The logo is part of this language but separate from it: see [`brand/identity/`](../identity/README.md) for the
logo system. This file covers everything around it.

---

## The signature

**Established** (identity README, guide sections 04–07): warm grounds, Earth Brown type and logo, one Terracotta
accent, a geometric sans with an editorial serif, and objects shown on their own surfaces (stone, terrazzo, kraft,
paper).

**Recommended**, the balances the language holds:

| Between                   | In practice                                                                           |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Geometric and organic     | Straight grids and square frames around irregular materials and soft light            |
| Architectural and human   | Planes, thresholds and alignment, with hands, use and small variations in the objects |
| Minimal and tactile       | Few elements, but each with texture: paper, stone, a debossed mark                    |
| Refined and imperfect     | Precise typography and layout; the objects carry the handmade variation               |
| Contemporary and timeless | Current in its clarity and restraint; no trend effects                                |
| Warm and restrained       | Warm neutrals everywhere; accent colour used once                                     |

A quick test: if a composition would still look right with the photograph removed and only the type left, the
structure is sound. If it only works because of an effect, it isn't ARANT.

## Graphic elements

The system has very few. **Established** unless noted:

1. **The symbol as texture** (guide section 07). Repeat the symbol on a loose grid, turning every second one upside
   down, in Sand on Ivory or Ivory on Sand. Keep it at least three times smaller than any logo on the same surface so
   it reads as texture, not a second logo. For tissue paper, box liners and web backgrounds.
2. **The band** (guide mockups: terracotta band on the kraft box; earth-brown wrapping band on tissue). A single
   strip of colour, often carrying the one-line lockup.
3. **The seal** (guide mockup). A circle in Terracotta with the reversed symbol and ring text. The accent element on
   a package.
4. **Hairline rules** (guide page). 1 px dividers in a light sand tint.
5. **Flat tinted plates** (guide page). Panels in a lighter tint of Ivory, or Sand, to group content without boxes.

**Recommended** limits:

- The pattern is a ground, never a foreground. On the web, use it only in small areas (a footer, an empty-state
  panel, a packaging-insert image), never behind reading text.
- No other patterns, illustrations, icons-as-decoration, or motifs.

## Icons

**Established:** the icon set in [`brand/identity/icons/`](../identity/icons/), generated by
`brand/identity/source/build_icons.py` (see the [identity README](../identity/README.md#icons)). Seventeen line icons:
search, bag, account, menu, close, arrow left and right, chevrons in four directions, plus, minus, check, alert,
expand (image zoom) and wishlist.

- A 24 px grid with a 2 px margin; one 1.5 stroke; rounded caps and joins to echo the mark; no fills.
- Drawn from the mark's vocabulary: straight strokes (the slabs), the circle (the pebble: the search lens, the
  account head, the two lobes of wishlist), the arch (the bag's handle, the account's shoulders), softened
  rectangles, and diagonals at 45°, the angle of the square grid.
- One colour, `currentColor`, so an icon takes the colour of the text beside it.
- At 16 px set `stroke-width` 2, at 20 px 1.75, so the drawn line stays about 1.3–1.5 px, the weight of Jost
  Regular beside it (the sprite reads `--icon-stroke`).

**Recommended:**

- Use as few icons as possible; words are often better (the mobile header's "Menu" button, filters as text).
- Pair an icon with a visible label or an accessible name; decorative icons get `aria-hidden`.
- The symbol is not an icon. Don't use it as a button, bullet, loader or rating star.
- Add an icon to the set (in `build_icons.py`, on the same grid and stroke) rather than borrowing one from another
  library.

## Indian in sensibility, contemporary in expression

**Recommended.** ARANT's Indianness comes from how things are made, the spaces they are made for and how the brand
speaks, not from applied motifs.

**Draw on:**

- **Architecture as structure.** Plinths, thresholds, wall niches, courtyards, stepwell geometry, arches read as
  openings rather than ornament. Use them as settings and proportions in photography and space, not as graphics.
- **Materials of Indian homes.** Stone floors, lime plaster, terrazzo, brass, clay, cane, handloom textiles, in
  photography and product styling.
- **Indian light.** Warm, strong daylight with long, soft-edged shadows; the quiet of a shaded interior.
- **Making.** Hands, workshops and small-batch processes, shown honestly.
- **Language and detail.** Rupee prices written as `₹1,800` (guide), Indian place names where they are true, and
  British English spelling.

**Never use, merely to signal "Indian":** mandalas, lotus motifs, paisley, temple silhouettes, elephants, rangoli
patterns, henna patterns, block-print borders, "exotic" colour clashes, or decorative scripts that imitate Devanagari.

## Future categories

Nothing in the visual language refers to a material or process. A ceramic vase, a wooden stool, a brass lamp or a
linen throw should sit in the same frame, on the same ground, with the same type, without any change to the system.
