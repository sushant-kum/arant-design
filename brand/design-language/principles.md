# Principles

Six principles every ARANT decision comes back to. Back to the [design-language index](README.md).

**Status.** The qualities are **Established**: the identity describes the mark as "architectural, tactile and quiet"
(`brand/identity/README.md`), and the planned principles were "architectural, tactile, understated, warm,
intentional" (the earlier version of this folder's README). The wording and the rules below are **Recommended**: a
first formalisation of those qualities.

Each principle comes from the mark itself. Balance is two equal slabs holding one pebble: few parts, one idea, even
openings, weight and support. The rest of the system should behave the same way.

---

## 1. One idea, held in balance

**Definition.** Every composition has one dominant idea (an object, a headline, an image) and everything else
supports it. Balance comes from weight and space, not from symmetry for its own sake.

- **Visual:** one focal element per composition. Asymmetry is allowed when the weights still balance, like the two
  slabs and the pebble.
- **UI:** one primary action and one dominant element per section. If two things compete, demote one.
- **Packaging:** one mark, one message and one accent per surface.
- **Photography:** one subject per frame; a second object only when it explains scale or use.
- **Example:** a product section with a single large photograph, a Newsreader headline and one text link. Not a
  photograph, three badges, a carousel and two buttons.
- **Avoid:** equal-weight grids of competing blocks; several accents on one surface; decoration added to fill space.

## 2. Space is part of the material

**Definition.** Whitespace is an active element, like the split and the pebble gap in the mark: it gives form to what
sits beside it.

- **Visual:** leave large calm areas. Let margins be generous and consistent rather than filled.
- **UI:** generous section spacing, a readable measure (about 64 characters), and room around every image. Density
  is never a goal in itself.
- **Packaging:** a small mark on a large plain surface reads as confident. Don't cover every face.
- **Photography:** negative space around the object; walls, floors and light count as composition.
- **Example:** a collection introduction of two sentences set alone on an ivory ground, with the grid starting well
  below it.
- **Avoid:** filling available space because it exists; shrinking gaps to fit more above the fold.

## 3. The object leads

**Definition.** The products and their materials carry the expression. The identity stays calm and controlled so they
can.

- **Visual:** the logo is small and quiet; photography and material do the talking.
- **UI:** large product imagery, minimal chrome, no decorative frames around images.
- **Packaging:** the object inside is the reveal; the box is a quiet, well-made container.
- **Photography:** the product is always the hero: sharp, well lit, true in colour and texture.
- **Example:** a hang tag with the symbol, the product name and the price, nothing more.
- **Avoid:** logos repeated on every surface; graphics laid over products; props that out-talk the object.

## 4. Structure before ornament

**Definition.** Architectural thinking: planes, slabs, openings, alignment and proportion do the work that ornament
would otherwise do.

- **Visual:** clear alignment, deliberate proportions, hairline rules rather than boxes.
- **UI:** a consistent grid, a shared left edge for text, hierarchy through size and space rather than colour or
  containers.
- **Packaging:** simple constructions (a box, a band, a seal) with clean, honest folds.
- **Photography:** architectural backgrounds (plaster walls, stone floors, thresholds, niches) and strong, simple
  geometry in the frame.
- **Example:** a product page where images, title and details share one grid, divided by space and a single rule.
- **Avoid:** decorative borders, flourishes, motifs used to signal "Indian", ornament for its own sake.

## 5. Warm through material, not effects

**Definition.** Warmth and tactility come from real surfaces, warm neutrals and light, never from simulated effects.

- **Visual:** warm neutral grounds (Warm Ivory, Sand); real textures in photography; the symbol pattern for subtle
  texture.
- **UI:** flat token colours, hairline borders, no gradients, glass effects or heavy shadows.
- **Packaging:** kraft, uncoated paper, deboss, stamp, one-colour print. The feel of the paper is part of the design.
- **Photography:** daylight and soft shadow; true material colour; no heavy grading.
- **Example:** a web banner that is a photograph of a stone surface with one line of type, instead of a gradient.
- **Avoid:** gradients, drop shadows, glossy finishes, glows, faux-paper backgrounds; rustic or "craft market" cues.

## 6. Made to last

**Definition.** Decisions should still look right in ten years and should work for any future ARANT object: a tray, a
ceramic vase, a wooden bench, a lamp.

- **Visual:** a small, stable vocabulary used consistently, not refreshed each season.
- **UI:** a few restrained components reused everywhere; subtle motion that serves the content.
- **Packaging:** a system that works for any product size and material, from a coaster to a lamp.
- **Photography:** a consistent light and surface language across categories and years.
- **Example:** a product-card layout that works equally for a cast tray and a turned wooden bowl, because nothing in
  it refers to casting.
- **Avoid:** trends (colours of the year, fashionable effects or typefaces); anything that ties the brand to one
  material, process or category.

---

## Using the principles

When a request is ambiguous, prefer the option that is more consistent, clearer, more timeless, more tactile, less
decorated and more scalable to future categories, in that order.
