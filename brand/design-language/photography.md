# Photography

Light, surfaces, composition and styling for product, in-context and process photography. Back to the
[design-language index](README.md).

**Status.** No photography exists in the repo yet; the brand guide's "In use" scenes are drawn in the browser, not
photographed. Everything here is **Recommended** unless marked.

---

## The rule that matters most

The product is the hero. Every choice of light, surface, prop and crop exists to show one object's form, material
and scale truthfully.

## Light

**Prefer:**

- Natural daylight from one side (a window or a door), or a single large soft source that imitates it.
- Soft directional light that shows surface texture and the edges of a form.
- Controlled, soft-edged shadows that anchor the object to its surface. A long late-afternoon shadow is welcome; a
  hard black shadow is not.
- Warm-neutral rendering: whites look like Warm Ivory, not blue-white. Calibrate white balance on a grey card, then
  warm slightly; never push to orange.

**Avoid:** on-camera flash, ring lights, hard studio strobes, seamless white sweeps with no shadow, heavy HDR,
neon or coloured gels, heavy vignettes.

## Surfaces and backgrounds

**Prefer:** stone (honed, not polished), lime or clay plaster, terrazzo, warm untreated wood, uncoated paper, kraft,
linen, concrete, architectural surfaces such as a wall niche, a step, a window ledge or a plinth.

**Avoid:** glossy laminates, high-gloss marble, patterned tiles that compete with the object, busy textiles, plain
pure-white backgrounds (except marketplace listings that demand them), fake "rustic" boards.

## Composition

- **One clear subject.** A second object only to show scale, a set, or use.
- **Negative space.** Leave at least a third of the frame calm, so text can sit on it if needed and the object
  breathes.
- **Editorial framing.** Eye-level or slightly above for context; straight-on or top-down for form and detail.
  Avoid dramatic tilted angles.
- **Close-ups of material.** One detail shot per product: an edge, a surface, the debossed mark, a join.
- **Architecture in the frame.** A vertical line of a door, the horizontal of a shelf, a threshold. Structure frames
  the object (principle 4).
- **Leave space for the logo** when an image may carry it: a calm area, or a solid panel (identity README "Don't").

## Styling

- Products placed as they would be used in a considered interior: a tray on a console with keys, a vase with a
  single stem, a coaster under a glass.
- Props are few, real and quiet: a book, a cup, a branch, a folded linen. One or two at most, in the palette's warm
  neutrals.
- Hands may appear, doing something real (placing, holding, making). No posed hand models.
- No text props, visible brands, plastic packaging, fake plants or seasonal kitsch.
- Styling doesn't change with the product's material; a ceramic, a wooden and a mineral-cast object share one world.

## Shot list per product

| Shot           | Purpose                                | Aspect (web) |
| -------------- | -------------------------------------- | ------------ |
| Hero           | Form, on a calm surface, soft shadow   | 4:5          |
| In context     | Scale and use in a considered interior | 4:5 or 3:2   |
| Detail         | Material, edge, finish, the mark       | 1:1 or 4:5   |
| Set or variant | Colours or sizes side by side          | 3:2          |
| Listing        | Clean ground for marketplaces          | 1:1          |

Process and studio photography follows the same light and restraint: workbenches, tools, hands and materials,
honestly lit, never staged as "artisan" theatre.

## Post-production

- Correct, don't grade. True material colour is non-negotiable: a customer should recognise the object on arrival.
- Gentle contrast; keep shadow detail. No filters, heavy grain, split toning or colour-grading presets.
- Retouch dust and blemishes on the background; don't retouch away the object's genuine variation.
- Export in sRGB. Keep masters in Git LFS (**Established**: root `.gitattributes`, `brand/mockups/README.md`).

## Avoid, in one line

Excessive props, aggressive studio lighting, glossy e-commerce clichés, generic stock photography, visual clutter,
excessive filters, and anything that makes a handmade object look mass-produced or a refined one look rustic.
